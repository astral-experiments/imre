# Standard stream buffering at the process boundary

On Linux, the pinned CPython 3.14.5 interpreter accepts a 17,000-byte `sys.stdout.write` on a pipe. If that descriptor points to `/dev/full`, the error arrives at `flush()`. A single 131,072-byte write on the same descriptor reaches the kernel immediately. Earlier Imre builds attempted the 17,000-byte write immediately because the binary buffer held 8 KiB.

`TextIOWrapper` groups small encoded writes in 8 KiB chunks. Its binary writer uses the descriptor’s preferred block size bounded to 128 KiB–8 MiB. CPython sets stdout’s line buffering from the terminal present at startup; stderr is line buffered. See the pinned [`_io.open`](https://github.com/python/cpython/blob/5607950ef232dad16d75c0cf53101d9649d89115/Modules/_io/_iomodule.c), [`TextIOWrapper.write`](https://github.com/python/cpython/blob/5607950ef232dad16d75c0cf53101d9649d89115/Modules/_io/textio.c), and [`BufferedWriter.write`](https://github.com/python/cpython/blob/5607950ef232dad16d75c0cf53101d9649d89115/Modules/_io/bufferedio.c).

The [probe](stream_buffer.py) temporarily puts `/dev/full` on descriptor 1 or 2, records the first failing operation, restores the descriptor, then retries the flush. The [process runner](../../native_sys.py) compares the status and both output streams with the stock interpreter for 46 sequences at each optimization level. Two additional terminal starts check LF and CR after descriptor 1 is replaced. The parent opens the pipes and terminal; each subject performs its own stream operations in its child. These are supplemental controls and add no occurrences to `test.test_sys`.

Standard error snapshots the codec named by `PYTHONIOENCODING` at native startup. Its error policy is always `backslashreplace`; `-I` ignores the setting. Both `sys.stderr` and `sys.__stderr__` use this encoder, including when the runtime writes an uncaught exception or a `SystemExit` message. UTF-8 with a signature emits one BOM per stream, including on an empty first write.

The [encoding probe](stderr_encoding.py) records stderr metadata, bytes and exit status against CPython 3.14.5. It exercises the native registry's ASCII, Latin-1, UTF-8 and UTF-8 with signature. It also runs two original `test_exit` children and checks the final diagnostic line of an uncaught exception. The [Linux results](stderr-encoding-linux.json) are supplemental. The full `test_exit` still stops at a lone surrogate; `test_ioencoding` also needs CP424 and configured stdout encoding.
